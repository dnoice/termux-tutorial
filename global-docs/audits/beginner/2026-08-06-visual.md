# Termux for Beginners — Rendered Visual Audit

**Scope.** What the built site actually looks like on screen, measured from rendered pages in Chrome across three viewports and both themes. Covers layout, colour, contrast ratios, type metrics, component geometry, and whether the interactive pieces read as working. Does not cover code quality, architecture, build config, accessibility semantics beyond what is visible, or content/copy editing — those belong to the separate code-level audit. Every hex, px and ratio below was read off a live render, not inferred from source.

**Audit date:** 2026-08-06

**Themes:** Sentinel Obsidian (dark, default) and Parchment (light).

**Viewports:** 1394×776 desktop, 768×1024 tablet, 390×844 and 360×800 phone.

## Executive summary

Sentinel Obsidian is genuinely handsome. The obsidian-and-brass palette is disciplined, the Crimson Pro over Source Serif 4 pairing is beautiful, the frosted header actually reads as glass over paper, and the card and panel construction — 12px radii, 1px hairlines, surfaces at 60-72% alpha so the scatter field ghosts through — is the work of someone with taste. Then a handful of cascade and token bugs undo a disproportionate amount of it. Two are contrast catastrophes that hit every page: the closing "Switch it on" CTA renders at 1.0:1 in dark and 1.06:1 in light because a Starlight prose-link rule out-specifies the button's own fill, and the active navigation item renders at 1.31:1 in dark and 1.04:1 in light, so on every lesson page the sidebar highlights a blank bar where the current page should be. The type system is also not applying its own stated intent: every lesson `h1` renders in Inter 42px rather than Crimson Pro, and `li` renders Inter while `p` renders Source Serif 4, so bulleted lists visibly switch face mid-prose. Parchment is a real, carefully-built light theme rather than an afterthought — but the whole Starlight accent layer was never bridged onto the Fire Watch tokens, so it currently carries electric navy, royal purple, magenta, green and cornflower blue alongside the brass, and `--color-brand-emphasis` has inverted polarity in light mode, dragging every prose link and inline code chip below WCAG AA. On a phone the site is structurally excellent — zero horizontal overflow anywhere, a measured CLS of exactly 0 despite two React terminal islands — but the live terminal, the site's entire promise, gets 39 columns at 390px against ~80-column authored output, and the 25-character prompt leaves a learner 14 characters before their own typing wraps mid-word. Nothing here is a rewrite. Six of the ten highest-impact fixes are one-line edits.

## Verdict

| Viewport | Grade | Justification |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Desktop — Dark | B- | A genuinely well-composed theme undercut by two 1.0:1 contrast failures on every page and an `h1` that is off the type system. |
| Desktop — Light | C+ | The foundation (canvas, prose, surfaces, frosted chrome) is excellent, but five competing accent hues leak through unbridged and inline code, links and both CTAs all fail AA. |
| Mobile | C | Structurally sound and shift-free, but the interactive terminal is column-starved to the point of being hard to follow, the current nav item is invisible, and no tap target on the site reaches 44px. |

### Desktop — Dark (Sentinel Obsidian)

#### D1. ✅ CLOSED — "Switch it on" CTA renders as near-black text on near-black canvas

> **FIXED & VERIFIED 2026-08-06.** Added `:not(.tmx-btn)` to the prose-link
> selector and its `:hover` twin in `global.css`, so the rule's `background`
> shorthand no longer wipes the button fill. Dropped the now-unnecessary
> `!important` on `a.tmx-btn--primary`'s `color`. Also set
> `font-family: var(--font-body)` + `font-size: 1rem` on the base `.tmx-btn`
> rule so buttons stop inheriting the body serif inside markdown content.
> Measured after fix: `background-image` = real brass gradient (was
> `0% 1.5px` collapsed underline), `color` = `rgb(250,247,242)` on brass,
> `padding` = symmetric `10.4px 21.6px` (was `10.4px 21.6px 1px`),
> height = **40px** (was 31px). Contrast 1.0:1 → passes.

**Severity:** Critical · **Effort:** Trivial · **Page:** Landing `/`, bottom CTA row (x=361-521, y=506-537 in viewport when scrolled to page bottom) · **Spans:** all three viewports, both themes

**Observed.** The button renders as a barely-perceptible dark smudge with a soft gold halo bleeding out of its bottom edge. Zoomed in, "Switch it on →" is legible only as slightly-darker-than-background lettering. Computed `background-color: rgba(0,0,0,0)` — no fill at all — with `background-image: linear-gradient(rgb(212,177,92), rgb(212,177,92))` sized `0% 1.5px` at position `0% 100%`. That is a collapsed link-underline sweep, not a button fill. `color: rgb(14,16,20)` sits on the `#0e1014` canvas: **1.0:1**. The only reason anything is visible is the surviving `box-shadow: color(srgb 0.831 0.694 0.361 / 0.7) 0 8px 22px -10px`. Padding computes to `10.4px 21.6px 1px` — bottom padding collapsed to 1px, so the text is jammed against the lower edge and the pill is 31px tall against the hero button's 56px. It also renders in Source Serif 4 16.32px where the hero button uses Inter 16px.

**Cause.** Specificity collision in `src/styles/global.css`. The prose-link rule at line 349, `.sl-markdown-content a:not(.card):not(.action):not(:where(.not-content *))`, sets the `background` **shorthand** at line 352 — which resets `background-size`, `-position` and `-repeat` — plus `padding-bottom: 1px` at line 353. `a.tmx-btn--primary` at line 855 is lower specificity and loses. `color` survives only because line 857 uses `!important`, which is precisely what makes the failure total: it keeps the on-gold ink after the gold is gone. The `<button>`-based `.tmx-btn--primary` instances ("Mark complete", "Boot Linux") are unaffected, which is why this reads as a one-off rather than systemic.

**Recommendation.** Add `:not(.tmx-btn)` to the prose-link selector at `global.css:349` and to its `:hover` twin at `:356`. That single edit restores the gradient, the bottom padding and the 40px height in one go. Then drop the `!important` on line 857, which is only masking the specificity problem. Verify computed `background-color` resolves to a real brass fill and padding returns to symmetric `10.4px 21.6px`. Audit every other `.tmx-btn` that lives inside `.sl-markdown-content` — the same rule is hitting all of them.

#### D2. ✅ CLOSED — Active sidebar item is illegible on every page

> **FIXED & VERIFIED 2026-08-06.** Took the recommended cheapest-correct path:
> kept the soft gradient pill and restated the ink on
> `.sidebar-content a[aria-current='page']` — `color: var(--fg-default)`,
> `font-weight: 600`, plus `border-left: 2px solid var(--color-brand)` so the
> active state never depends on the low-contrast wash alone. Also reset the
> nested `.sl-badge` inside `[aria-current='page']` to
> `background: transparent; color: inherit; border-color: currentColor`.
> Measured after fix (light): `color` = `rgb(44,36,24)` (was `rgb(245,240,232)`
> at 1.04:1), weight 600, brass 2px left rail present. The 0.14-alpha ink is
> gone from both themes.

**Severity:** Critical · **Effort:** Trivial · **Page:** every page with a sidebar (`/start/why-termux/`, `/foundations/storage/`, `/foundations/packages/`, `/progress/`, `/reference/cheatsheet/`) · **Spans:** all three viewports, both themes

**Observed.** The current page's sidebar entry renders as a washed-out gold lozenge whose text all but disappears into it — "Why Termux (not the Play Store)", "Bridging Android Storage" and "Command Cheatsheet" are all *harder* to read than the inactive items around them, which is exactly backwards. Computed `color: rgba(212, 177, 92, 0.14)` — the `--color-brand-muted` token being used as a **text** colour. Composited over the `--tmx-grad-soft` background (≈`rgb(62,58,45)`) the ink resolves to ≈`rgb(83,75,52)`: **1.31:1** against its own background, versus 4.5:1 for AA. Inactive siblings render a healthy `rgb(181,172,153)`. On `/foundations/storage/` the nested "Interactive" badge inherits the same wash. In light mode the same rule paints `rgb(245,240,232)` on a cream-tinted pill for **1.04:1** — a completely blank lozenge — and in the mobile flyout it is ~1.1:1 dark and ~1.2:1 light.

**Cause.** `global.css:529` — `.sidebar-content a[aria-current='page'] { background: var(--tmx-grad-soft); border-radius: var(--radius-md); }` replaces Starlight's solid accent fill with a ~20%-alpha gradient but never restates `color`. Starlight's default current-item colour was chosen to sit on a **solid** accent fill and resolves through the theme bridge (`global.css:181`, `--sl-color-accent-low: var(--color-brand-muted)`) to the 14%-alpha brass. In light the same on-emphasis token resolves through `--sl-color-black: var(--bg-canvas)` to `#f5f0e8`. The background was restyled; the ink was not.

**Recommendation.** Decide which half is authoritative and match the other to it. Cheapest correct fix: keep the soft gradient pill and set `color: var(--fg-default)` on the rule at `global.css:529` — that yields roughly 8:1 in dark and ~10:1 in light. Keep `font-weight: 600` and add `border-left: 2px solid var(--color-brand)` so the active state does not depend on the low-contrast wash alone. The alternative is to make the pill genuinely solid (`background: var(--color-brand)`) and keep `--fg-on-emphasis`. Whichever you pick, also reset the nested badge inside `[aria-current='page']` to `background: transparent; color: inherit; border-color: currentColor`, and add a contrast assertion for `aria-current="page"` in both themes. Do not ship the 0.14 alpha under any circumstance.

#### D3. ✅ CLOSED — Lesson-page `h1` renders in Inter, not Crimson Pro

> **FIXED & VERIFIED 2026-08-06.** Added `.content-panel h1, h1#_top` to the
> Crimson Pro heading rule so Starlight's page-frame title (rendered outside
> `.sl-markdown-content`) picks up the display family. Also took the secondary
> recommendation and strengthened the hierarchy:
> `font-size: clamp(2.1rem, 4vw, 2.85rem); line-height: 1.1;
> letter-spacing: -0.02em; font-weight: 700`.
> Measured after fix: **Crimson Pro Variable, 45.6px, 700** (was Inter 42px 600),
> now a clear step above the 35px `h2` and matching the landing `h1`'s voice.

**Severity:** High · **Effort:** Small · **Page:** every lesson and reference page · **Spans:** all three viewports, both themes

**Observed.** "Why Termux (not the Play Store)", "Bridging Android Storage", "Package Management with pkg", "Your Progress" and "Command Cheatsheet" all render in a geometric sans, while the `h2` directly beneath each one renders in serif. The family switch is obvious on screen. Computed `h1`: **Inter Variable, 42px, 600, 50.4px line-height, `rgb(232,223,204)`, letter-spacing normal**. Computed markdown `h2`: **Crimson Pro Variable, 35px, 700, 42px, -0.35px**. The landing-page `h1` is correct — **Crimson Pro Variable, 57.6px, 700, 60.48px, -0.576px** — which isolates the fault to the page-frame title. Secondary problem: at 42px the lesson `h1` is only 7px larger than its own `h2`, so with the family already differing the hierarchy reads weak. At 390px it is worse: `h1` computes to Inter 35px/600 against `h2` at Crimson Pro 24px/700, and on `/foundations/packages/` the title occupies 165px (three lines) of the first screen.

**Cause.** The landing `h1` lives inside `.sl-markdown-content` and picks up the Crimson Pro heading rule. Starlight's lesson-page `<h1 id="_top">` is rendered by the page-frame component outside that scope and falls through to `--sl-font` (Inter). The theme bridge maps colours onto Starlight tokens but never maps the heading family onto the `h1` outside markdown content.

**Recommendation.** Extend the Crimson Pro heading rule to Starlight's page title — target `.content-panel h1, h1#_top`, or set the heading family at the layout level on `h1,h2,h3,h4` and let the UI-chrome headings (sidebar group labels, "On this page") opt back into Inter explicitly. While there, raise the lesson `h1` to ~44-48px with `letter-spacing: -0.02em` and `font-weight: 700` so it matches the landing `h1`'s voice and opens a clear step above the 35px `h2`.

#### D4. ✅ CLOSED — List items render in Inter while paragraphs render in Source Serif 4

> **FIXED & VERIFIED 2026-08-06.** Added an explicit
> `.sl-markdown-content :is(p, li, td, th, dd, blockquote)` rule setting
> `font-family: var(--font-detail)`, and aligned the metrics with
> `.sl-markdown-content li { font-size: 1.02rem; line-height: 1.7; }`.
> Measured after fix: `li` = **Source Serif 4 Variable, 16.32px, 400** — a byte-for-byte
> match with `p` on the same page. Lists and paragraphs now share a baseline
> rhythm; the mid-prose face switch is gone.

**Severity:** High · **Effort:** Trivial · **Page:** `/start/why-termux/` (plugin list y=322-423, numbered steps y=157-223), `/foundations/packages/` (Recap bullets y=286-352), `/foundations/storage/` · **Spans:** all viewports, both themes

**Observed.** Bulleted and numbered lists visibly shift to a sans face against the serif paragraphs around them. On `/start/why-termux/` the item "Termux:API — access the camera, GPS, battery, and more" at y=322 is sans, while the paragraph immediately below at y=467 is serif. Computed `li`: **Inter Variable, 16px, 400, 28px, `rgb(181,172,153)`**. Computed `p`: **Source Serif 4 Variable, 16.32px, 400, 27.744px, `rgb(181,172,153)`**. Same colour, same size band, different family — which reads as an accident rather than a deliberate distinction.

**Cause.** The body-serif rule is scoped to `p`, `td` and `th` — all three of which correctly render Source Serif 4 at 16.32px — but `li` was never included, so list items fall through to the inherited `--sl-font`. The fact that `td`/`th` were covered makes `li` look like a simple omission.

**Recommendation.** Add `li` to the rule that sets `font-family: var(--font-body)` on `p`/`td`/`th`, and align the metrics: `font-size: 16.32px` (or the same rem value `p` uses) with `line-height: 27.744px`, so list and paragraph text share a baseline rhythm.

#### D5. ✅ CLOSED — Aside titles are unthemed Starlight defaults that clash with their own rails

> **FIXED & VERIFIED 2026-08-06.** Extended the theme bridge to Starlight's
> semantic hue ramp so rails and titles finally resolve from one source:
> `--sl-color-blue/green/orange/red/purple` (+ their `-low`/`-high` companions)
> now map to `--color-info/success/warning/critical/accent`, and
> `--sl-color-asides-text-accent` maps to `--fg-default`. Also switched
> `.starlight-aside__title` to `var(--font-heading)` (it was rendering the *body*
> serif on a heading role). Danger and caution keep a tinted title via
> `color-mix(... 55%, var(--fg-default))` because in a tutorial that warns people
> off the Play Store those two carry a real safety signal.
> Measured after fix (light): tip and note titles both `rgb(44,36,24)` at
> **14.32:1**, rendering **Crimson Pro Variable**, with semantic rails intact
> (tip green `rgb(61,122,69)`, note blue `rgb(74,111,165)`). The purple-on-green
> and pink-on-red pairings are gone; no foreign accent leaks remain.

**Severity:** High · **Effort:** Small · **Page:** `/start/why-termux/` (danger, caution), `/foundations/packages/` (tip, note), `/foundations/storage/` (tip, danger), `/progress/` (note) · **Spans:** all viewports, both themes; far louder in light

**Observed.** Three of four aside variants pair a rail in one hue family with a title in a completely different one. Dark theme measurements: **tip** — rail `rgb(95,181,115)` green, title `rgb(235,204,250)` lilac. **note** — rail `rgb(127,182,224)` blue, title `rgb(189,195,255)` periwinkle. **danger** — rail `rgb(217,78,58)` red, title `rgb(249,195,214)` pink. Only **caution** is coherent: rail `rgb(212,177,92)` brass, title `rgb(249,232,195)` warm cream. On screen "The Play Store build is broken by design" reads pink above a red bar, and "Always update before installing" reads violet above a green bar. Light theme is worse because the hues are fully saturated against cream: tip title `rgb(102,8,145)` royal purple over a `rgb(61,122,69)` green rail; note title `rgb(15,28,138)` electric navy over a `rgb(74,111,165)` blue rail; danger title `rgb(138,15,58)` magenta over a `rgb(178,34,34)` firebrick rail that matches neither the title nor `--color-danger` (`#8b2d2d`). Secondary: aside titles render **Source Serif 4 Variable 18px 600** — the *body* font on a heading-role element, where the system otherwise uses Crimson Pro.

**Cause.** The theme bridge maps Starlight's aside **rail** colours onto the Fire Watch semantic tokens (`--color-critical` etc., `global.css:88-91`) but never overrides `--sl-color-blue`, `--sl-color-purple`, `--sl-color-red`, `--sl-color-green` or `--sl-color-asides-text-accent`, which is what `.starlight-aside__title` resolves against. So rail and title now come from two different sources.

**Recommendation.** Extend the bridge to cover Starlight's semantic hues so both halves resolve from one place: `--sl-color-blue` → `var(--color-info)`, `--sl-color-purple` → `var(--color-accent)`, `--sl-color-green` → `var(--color-success)`, `--sl-color-red` → `var(--color-danger)`, `--sl-color-orange` → `var(--color-warning)`, plus their `-low` companions. Then make each title a light tint of its own rail, matching how caution already works — e.g. `.starlight-aside--danger .starlight-aside__title { color: color-mix(in srgb, var(--color-critical) 45%, var(--fg-default)); }`. Given that brass is the single stated warm accent, the stronger option is brass rails with `--fg-default` titles for note and tip, reserving semantic colour for danger and caution where it carries a real safety signal in a tutorial that tells people not to install from the Play Store. At minimum, kill the purple-on-green tip. Also switch `.starlight-aside__title` to `var(--font-display)`.

#### D7. ✅ CLOSED — Scatter-field shell text collides with prose baselines

> Took the recommended fix rather than dimming --tmx-bg-opacity, so the pattern keeps full
> strength in the margins where it reads best. `.sl-markdown-content` and `.hero .copy`
> now paint `color-mix(in srgb, var(--bg-canvas) 70%, transparent)` behind their own text.
> Two deliberate implementation choices: (1) it is a pseudo-element, not a `background` on
> the column, because the column's box model must not move — the mobile full-bleed
> terminal resolves `calc(50% - 50vw)` against that width; (2) `z-index: 0` on the parent
> makes the column a self-contained stacking context so the -1 pseudo can never fall
> behind `body::before` at -2. All four edges are feathered with a two-layer mask
> (`mask-composite: intersect`, webkit `source-in`) so the wash reads as atmosphere and
> not a rectangle, and the inline inset is tied to `--sl-content-pad-x` so it reaches the
> content panel's edge and never further — a wider negative inset would add scrollable
> overflow and hand the phone a horizontal scrollbar. Header, sidebar and both terminal
> surfaces are untouched.

**Severity:** Medium · **Effort:** Small · **Page:** all pages; worst on the landing hero (tagline y=288-362), `/start/why-termux/` y=380-390, `/reference/cheatsheet/` table rows y=369-390 · **Spans:** all viewports; different failure mode in light (L5) and mobile (M7)

**Observed.** Background monospace shell text runs directly through body copy at the same optical size. On the landing hero, `drwxr-xr-x 2 user user 4096 May 12 10:15 projects` sits exactly on the baseline of "a live terminal you drive right here in the browser." and `$ ls -la` sits in the leading between lines 2 and 3. On the cheatsheet the same string runs through the `whoami` / "Show your username" row. To be precise about severity: this is **not** a contrast failure. The SVG ink is `#b7c5d3` at 0.06-0.10 opacity over `#0e1014`, giving a busiest-case background of ≈`rgb(31,34,39)`; body text at `rgb(181,172,153)` measures **8.50:1** over plain canvas and still **7.08:1** over the busiest patch. It is a figure-ground *noise* problem: competing text at a similar size and stroke weight in the same region, which the eye has to actively suppress. Most damaging in the wide dead zones beside short tables and beside the hero, where the pattern has nothing to sit behind.

**Cause.** `body::before` (`global.css:246-258`) paints the scatter field at `opacity: var(--tmx-bg-opacity, 1)` — full strength. The `body::after` scrim (`global.css:263-280`) is `radial-gradient(120% 95% at 50% 45%, transparent 55%, …)`, which darkens only the **edges** and is fully transparent through the centre 55% — precisely where the text column sits. The comment at `global.css:260-262` asserts that "no heavy legibility scrim is needed", which holds for the geometric motifs but not for the literal shell-text glyphs.

**Recommendation.** Cheapest high-impact fix: drop `--tmx-bg-opacity` to ~0.5-0.6 for dark (`global.css:110`). Better: give the reading column its own scrim — `background: color-mix(in srgb, var(--bg-canvas) 70%, transparent)` behind `.sl-markdown-content` and the hero copy block — so the pattern survives in the margins, where it looks best, but never sits under running text. Optionally split the SVG so the literal shell-text glyphs are the layer that fades while the geometric icons stay at full strength.

#### D8. ✅ CLOSED — Theme dropdown is the only pill in the header and 13px taller than its neighbours

> Replaced Starlight-s <select> with a single cycling button (src/components/overrides/ThemeSelect.astro). Measured after: 40x40 with border-radius 8px at top 12 — byte-identical geometry to the search field it sits beside (was 52px tall, border-radius 9999px, top 6: the tallest element in a 64px header and the only pill in a UI whose radii are 8/10/12px). One 18px icon replaces two icons that were 14px at opacity 1.0 and 20px at opacity 0.7. Cycles light -> dark -> System, verified by clicking through all three states; aria-label announces current and next. Also fixed a latent bug found during verification: Starlight renders ThemeSelect TWICE (desktop header + mobile menu), so a fixed id was duplicated and only the first instance was wired — the mobile toggle was dead. Now class-based with a delegated listener.

**Severity:** Medium · **Effort:** Small · **Page:** header, all pages (x=1254-1355, y=5-58) · **Desktop only**

**Observed.** In a 64px header: theme control **53px** tall, `border-radius: 9999px`, top at y=5. Search button **40px**, `border-radius: 8px`, top at y=12. GitHub icon **32px**, top at y=16. ## Theme parity

Parchment is not a token flip of Sentinel Obsidian; it is a separately-designed theme with its own scatter-field asset, its own surface stack and its own frosted-chrome tuning, and the foundation measures well above AA. Three specific classes of thing break when the theme flips.

**1. On-emphasis foreground assumptions invert.** Anything written as "the text that sits on an emphasis surface is dark" flips to cream when `--bg-canvas` goes from `#0e1014` to `#f5f0e8`. This is the shared root of the two worst bugs on the site: the active nav item (D2 — 1.31:1 dark, **1.04:1 light**) and the closing CTA (D1 — 1.0:1 dark, **1.06:1 light**). It is also why the nested "Local" badge inside an active row loses its fill entirely in light. The pattern to kill: never use `--bg-canvas` as the foreground on a translucent-tint surface. Either make the surface genuinely solid and keep `--fg-on-emphasis`, or keep the tint and use `--fg-default`.

**2. Token polarity inverts.** `--color-brand-emphasis` is defined by lightness in both themes, which is correct in dark (lighter = further from a dark canvas) and backwards in light (lighter = closer to a cream canvas). One token definition drags prose links to 3.35:1, inline code to 3.18:1 and the active TOC entry to 3.35:1 (L1, L2). Redefining emphasis as *distance from canvas* rather than *lighter* fixes all of them in a single edit — and is the highest value-per-character change in this report after the two `:not()` additions.

**3. Everything Starlight owns leaks through unbridged, and the leak is invisible in dark.** The theme bridge maps colours, greys and hairlines but never maps `--sl-color-blue`, `--sl-color-purple`, `--sl-color-red`, `--sl-color-green` or the aside title accents. In dark those defaults are pastels sitting on obsidian and merely look odd (D5, D13). In light they resolve to fully saturated electric navy `#0f1c8a`, royal purple `#660891`, magenta `#8a0f3a` and green `#3d7a45`, plus a stock Night Owl Light code theme adding cornflower blue `#3b61b0` (L6). Against a brief that names brass as the single warm accent, Parchment currently carries **five competing accent hues**, and a mobile reader meets navy, purple and magenta headings within two screens of scrolling. Dark theme hid this problem rather than not having it.

Two smaller parity notes. Permanently-dark surfaces — LiveSandbox, the terminals — must draw from dark-locked tokens; `--tmx-screen` and `--tmx-screen-ink` already do this correctly in both blocks, but `--fg-muted` and `--color-brand` used inside those surfaces do not (L3). And the border tokens were tuned for a dark canvas, so on cream the header, code blocks and TOC all sit near the floor of perceptibility (L11); light mode needs a slightly heavier hairline step, not the same hex.

**Parity verdict:** the light theme is one token redefinition and one bridge extension away from being as strong as the dark theme. Neither is a redesign.

## Mobile reality check

The site's core promise is an interactive terminal you drive from your phone. The honest assessment: **it works, and it is hard to use.**

What genuinely works, and works well:

- The terminal accepts touch input correctly. Tapping focuses `.xterm-helper-textarea`, which
  carries `autocapitalize=off`, `autocorrect=off`, `spellcheck=false` and `font-size: 16.32px`.
  That last value is what prevents iOS Safari's focus-zoom, and someone chose it deliberately.
  This is better mobile input hygiene than most terminal embeds ship with.
- Fish-style autosuggestion works at phone width — typing `ls ~/sto` correctly rendered `rage`
  as a dimmed completion.
- Nothing shifts. CLS measured **0.0000** on a page mounting two `client:only` React terminal
  islands, because the height is reserved up front. That is a real engineering win and it must
  survive any fix to the terminal sizing.
- Nothing overflows. Zero horizontal document overflow at 360, 390 and 768px on all six pages.
- The background is implemented the mobile-safe way and does not jank on scroll.

What makes it hard to use, in order:

- **39 columns.** At 390px the terminal is 39 columns wide; the prompt is 25 of them. A learner
  gets 14 characters before their own typing wraps mid-token. The taught command
  `termux-setup-storage` breaks as `termux-setup-s` / `torage` — a beginner cannot tell whether
  the break is real. Aligned symlink output loses its alignment entirely, which is the whole
  point of that output. At 360px it is 35 columns and worse.
- **110px of dead black below the last row**, roughly 13% of an 844px viewport, at the same time
  the terminal is horizontally starved. It reads as a broken or still-loading widget.
- **The flagship WebVM button is 85×53 at 12px** and wraps to "Boot" / "Linux", under a title
  that has itself wrapped, above a developer-facing cross-origin-isolation error shown verbatim
  to beginners.
- **The current page is invisible in the nav flyout** in both themes, so a learner navigating
  between lessons on a phone has no "you are here".
- **Nothing is 44×44.** The progress checkbox is 25×18 and sits beside a link that navigates
  away, so a miss costs the learner their place.

The fixable part is the column starvation, and it is more fixable than it looks. Shortening the sandbox prompt from `u0_a123 at localhost ~ ❯` to `~ ❯` alone returns 22 columns — going from 14 usable characters to 36 — and is a string change, not a layout change. Add full-bleed framing below 480px and a 12px font at that breakpoint and the terminal reaches ~55 columns, which is enough for the lessons as authored. Do all three and the mobile terminal goes from "technically works" to "actually teachable" without touching the CLS-preserving height reservation that makes the page feel solid today.
